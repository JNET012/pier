What this demonstration is, and what it is not
  • The rooms are stand ins. The Great Hall is traced from a demonstration drawing and sized so the planner's own table counts match the venue's published capacity: up to 650 guests for a banquet in rounds of 12, and 600 to 650 for cabaret in rounds of 8. The Regency Suite is invented. In a real build each room is traced from the architect's record drawing using the dimensions printed on it, then checked against that drawing and on site.
  • The companies are invented. Any resemblance to real businesses is coincidence.
  • Position scores are placeholders. Distance from the stage and from service doors are guesses. In a real build the events manager ranks the tables and the weights are worked back from that.
  • Table spacing is automatic. The planner works out the widest comfortable gap at which the tables you need will fit, spreads them evenly, and keeps them clear of fixed rooms and features, columns, doors and exits. When more tables are needed it re spaces the whole room. The spacing limits here are placeholders: 1.5 m between table edges is treated as comfortable, and the planner will go as tight as 1.25 m before it tells you the room is full. Tables also stay at least 1.2 m clear of any door opening.
  • The planner runs in your browser. It seats everyone, keeps each company's tables in one connected block, honours accessible seating and locked tables, and when numbers change it moves as little as it can. If a change will not fit around the old plan it first adds a table if the room is short of spares, then plans the night again, and only splits a company when the room genuinely cannot hold it in one block, saying so when it does. The build version would use an exact solver, and could also let two small companies share a table when a room is full.
  • It does not decide how many people may be in a room. That depends on exits, escape routes, the fire risk assessment and the premises licence. Enter the approved capacity and the planner warns you when a layout goes over it. Every layout needs sign off by a competent person.
  • Not shown here: planning across several rooms on one night, reading bookings straight from iVvy or the ticketing system, and exact dietary counts for each table. That last one comes from the pre event form.
